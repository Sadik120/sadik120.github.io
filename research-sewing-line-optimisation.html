Integrating Exact Line Balancing, Stochastic Simulation and Multi-Criteria Selection with Lean Improvement: Evidence from a Luggage Sewing Line

Md Sadekul Islam and Mohammad Ali Sabuj · Department of Industrial Engineering and Management, KUET

Why this question

Studies of apparel sewing lines usually compare a few priority-rule heuristics on one line and recommend whichever gives the highest line efficiency. This paper returns to the 27-operation Duffle Trolley bag line at VIP Industries Bangladesh from my B.Sc. study and asks whether that choice still matters once designs are checked against exact optima, exposed to task-time variability and screened against demand.

Method

A five-stage framework, with every model coded in Python:

  • Balance: four heuristics (largest candidate rule, Kilbridge–Wester, most number of followers and ranked positional weight) against exact SALBP-1, SALBP-2 and parallel-station integer programmes, all solved to proven optimality in seconds
  • Simulate: discrete-event simulation of every design under lognormal task-time variability and finite buffers, 30 replications per case
  • Screen: keep only designs whose 95% lower confidence bound on throughput reaches the 480-unit shift target
  • Rank: TOPSIS and VIKOR, with 10,000 random weight vectors to test whether the ranking holds
  • Lean feedback: measured kaizen and 5S savings (11.7%) are fed back into the optimisation

Findings

  • Two operations (1.20 and 1.25 min) exceed the 1.0 min takt time, so every heuristic is capped at 80% of demand
  • Ranked positional weight already matches the exact SALBP-1 optimum, so the choice of heuristic is a second-order question
  • Exact parallel-station balancing meets takt with 16 operators under fixed times but falls 1–4% short under variability. Planning at 95% of takt with 17 operators and two-unit buffers restores demand
  • Without demand screening, TOPSIS would favour a design that misses demand by 25% for about half of the weight space
  • Lean savings bring the robust design down to 15 operators under every allocation tested: 31% more output and 110% higher labour productivity than the current 24-operator line

Resolving indivisible bottlenecks and planning with a capacity margin matter more than the choice of heuristic.

Citation

Research

My research uses optimization, simulation and learning-based methods to help engineers design production systems, from balancing a single line to laying out a whole facility.

M.Engg. thesis · BUET · September 2026 · Manuscript in preparation

Integrating Evolutionary Algorithm and Deep Reinforcement Learning for Efficient Facility Layout Design

Abstract: Facility layout decides how far materials travel inside a factory, and it has to respect machine sizes, safety space and the flow between machines. This thesis builds a two-stage pipeline for the problem. A genetic algorithm first generates feasible layouts, and a Rainbow DQN agent then refines them. The agent works with a 12-component composite reward, action masking, dimension-aware prevention of overlaps across multiple grid cells, and a graph neural network encoder pre-trained on synthetic material-flow graphs. The method is evaluated on a 26-machine industrial layout from a Bangladeshi manufacturer, with sensitivity analyses over reward weights (a 21-run design at two search budgets), grid resolution (9, 11 and 13) and flow density (six flow configurations). The thesis also documents the problems that remain open: training instability, residual overlaps, and unreliable outcomes when flow data are very sparse. These set the next steps: multi-seed evaluation, longer training and constraint-aware repair.

Journal article · 2019 · American Journal of Industrial and Business Management

Production Efficiency Improvement by Using Tecnomatix Simulation Software and RPWM Line Balancing Technique: A Case Study

Abstract: This case study, based on my B.Sc. thesis at KUET, looks at a sewing line in a bag manufacturing plant. Every operation on the line was time-studied, and the operations were then regrouped into workstations with the Ranked Positional Weight Method (RPWM) to balance the work across the line. The balanced line was modelled in Siemens Tecnomatix Plant Simulation to check that it was feasible before any change was made on the floor.
